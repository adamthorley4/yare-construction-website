# Yare Construction — Brand & Creative Brief

## Positioning
Premium design-and-build construction firm serving Norfolk / East Anglia, with the River Yare regional identity carried through copy (not a placeless national brand). Fictional demo build for Creek's own portfolio/marketing — see plan.md.

## Aesthetic direction: Bold Industrial
Raw construction materials treated as luxury: steel, corten/weathered metal, poured concrete, timber, glass. Matte, precise, engineered — not shiny/gilded luxury (differentiates from Mahari) and not cold-steel-grey (differentiates from KŌAN).

## Palette
- `--soot: #15130F` — near-black, warm undertone, primary background
- `--corten: #B4502A` — weathered/oxidised steel orange, primary accent
- `--chalk: #EDE8DF` — warm off-white, primary text on dark
- `--steel: #6B6A63` — mid warm grey, secondary text/dividers/labels

## Typography
- Display (hero, section headings, stats): **Big Shoulders Display**, 800/900 weight, uppercase, tight tracking — condensed industrial letterforms evoking steel signage/scaffold boards. Distinctive, unused elsewhere in the portfolio.
- Body: **Public Sans**, 300/400 — functional, precise grotesk.
- Labels/mono accents (spec sheets, stats units, nav): **IBM Plex Mono**, 400, uppercase, letterspaced.

## Hero concept: "Raw to Refined"
Single fixed camera position, three-quarter low angle, across a reed-fringed Norfolk riverbank toward a contemporary two-storey glass-and-timber extension.
- **Start frame:** pre-dawn blue hour. Structure is an exposed steel portal frame skeleton — scaffolding, tarpaulin, studwork, work lights on, mist over the water.
- **End frame:** same camera position, golden dusk. Structure complete — full-height glazing, warm timber cladding, corten steel accent panel, interior lights glowing amber, scaffolding gone.
No camera movement in either prompt — matches the site's own literal narrative (construction → completion) with zero interpolation motion artifacts.

## Copy tone
Confident, precise, technical-premium — not folksy or "workmanlike." Testimonials stay generic/placeholder attribution (e.g. "Homeowner, Norfolk"), never a fabricated named person presented as real, per plan.md's honest-marketing constraint. Trust badges (years trading, insurance) are invented but plausible — no real accreditation body logos.
